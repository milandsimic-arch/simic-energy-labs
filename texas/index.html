The 30-second answer

that can stop the project.

A low land price cannot solve missing fuel, unavailable power or an unworkable permit. Clear these gates before spending heavily on design.

01 / DECISION GATE

Power you can actually use

A power line is a lead. A deliverable MW commitment is evidence.

What evidence do we need?

Request the serving utility, study results, energization date, upgrade scope, costs and operating restrictions. For an islanded plant, prove reliability, black start and fuel continuity. Confirm the grid operator; not all Texas sites are in ERCOT.

02 / DECISION GATE

Gas that reaches the plant

Enough gas, at the right pressure, on the day you need it.

What evidence do we need?

Obtain the pipeline operator’s written flow and pressure study, firm transport terms, gas quality, tap and lateral scope, easements and construction schedule. A pipeline easement does not grant a right to take gas.

03 / DECISION GATE

Land you can build on

Gross acreage is the starting point. Usable acreage decides the layout.

What evidence do we need?

Commission a survey and test fit for compute halls, generation, cooling, setbacks, roads, drainage, laydown and expansion. Screen flood zones, wetlands, geology, easements and mineral access. No single acreage cutoff fits every architecture.

04 / DECISION GATE

Permission to operate

A buildable parcel still needs an approvable plant.

What evidence do we need?

Have an air consultant define the permitting path, emissions controls, modeling, public process and realistic schedule. Check local land use, noise, fire protection, water and discharge obligations. Do not assume “no zoning” means no permits.

05 / DECISION GATE

Cooling and connections

Heat must leave. Data must move. Both need designed routes.

What evidence do we need?

Request a peak-summer heat and water balance covering generation and compute. Secure water rights and supply where required. Seek carrier route maps, diverse entrances, construction costs and tested latency. Closed-loop liquid cooling still needs heat rejection.

06 / DECISION GATE

A deal that survives diligence

Buy a workable delivery schedule, not just attractive acreage.

What evidence do we need?

Resolve title, minerals, access, site control and development rights. Price the gas lateral, substation, roads, fiber, ground improvement and cooling. Compare all-in cost and time-to-power before ranking by asking price.

Illustrated insight

A data center is a chain of promises.

Gas becomes power. Power becomes computation. Almost all of that electrical energy becomes heat.

That is why the best parcel cannot be selected from acreage and transmission maps alone. Fuel, electrical architecture, cooling, communications and permissions must work together at the same load and on the same schedule.

What does 100 MW mean?

Choose the meaning of MW first. Power-plant output and IT load are different numbers.

100 MWCampus electrical supply
80 MWIT load supported at selected PUE
16.8–24.0MMcf/day gas · illustrative range
See the calculation and its limits

Gas = net campus MW × 24 × heat rate ÷ 1,000. Assumed net heat rate: 7–10 MMBtu/MWh; assumed gas heating value: 1,000 Btu/scf; 24-hour full output. These are scenario inputs, not a selected equipment guarantee. Redundancy, summer derating, reserve margin and transient response need a separate plant design. Gas volume changes with actual gas quality.

PUE includes cooling and other facility overhead. A 100 MW campus at PUE 1.25 supports approximately 80 MW of IT load. Land and water requirements need a site-specific layout and heat balance; this tool does not certify either.
