Methodology: weighted average, evidence coverage and gaps

Weighted average: sum of (criterion rating × criterion weight) divided by included weight. The default substitutes 3 for the draft’s “3?” values. Conservative mode substitutes 1. Rated-only mode excludes unknown criteria and renormalizes the remaining weights. These alternative modes are sensitivity scenarios, not new factual ratings.

Assigned-rating coverage: the share of total weight with a numerical rating in the draft. This is not the percentage of independently verified evidence. The attachment does not supply a separate citation and rationale for every individual numeric cell; available support is the property-level listing link and draft narrative.

Gates: reproduced separately from the attachment so a weighted score cannot conceal a draft failure. Its acreage gate is a screening assumption, not a universal engineering standard. Current site-control status and a project-specific layout can change the result.

Profile differences: SEL weights total 100. Original brief weights total 98 and are normalized to 100%; the source maps climate to water, roads/workforce to access, tax to deal, and omits its separate security factor. The OpenAI profile totals 100. The twelve technical chapters remain available; reliability and climate do not have separate star columns in the draft’s ten-factor model.

From curiosity to commitment

Every stage should buy a better answer.

The stages below are a planning sequence, not fixed durations or quoted fees. Work can overlap; the evidence should remain traceable.

01

Broker + project lead

Desk screen

Confirm availability, exact parcel and transaction terms. Identify the utility, pipeline operator and major physical constraints.

What you should have at the end

A tract identity, source log and explicit list of unknowns.

Pause if the seller cannot establish availability or identify the parcel.

02

Utility + gas operator + specialists

Written feasibility

Obtain power and gas scope, carrier routes, preliminary permit advice and title/surface review.

What you should have at the end

Dated counterpart responses, a concept architecture and a first enabling-cost range.

Pause if the required service cannot be delivered on a workable schedule.

03

Counsel + owner + project team

Conditional site control

Negotiate access and study rights, option conditions and an engineering scope aligned to the decision deadline.

What you should have at the end

An executed agreement and a funded, time-bounded diligence plan.

Do not let an option deadline force an unresolved technical assumption into a purchase decision.

04

Engineering + environmental + contractors

Field validation

Complete survey, borings, environmental work, layout, equipment logistics and refined project costs.

What you should have at the end

A decision package with residual risks, commercial commitments and a commissioning path.

Advance only when the agreed investment gates are met; resolve exceptions explicitly.

Method & definitions

What this guide does—and what comes next.

How were the two proposed designs improved?

This edition combines the Claude draft’s detailed tract analysis with the Muse draft’s plain-language criteria. It replaces speculative weighted stars with evidence labels, separates the visitor journey into three pages, and adds fresh listing checks. Five advertised properties form the reviewed comparison set. All 21 original leads are preserved in a separate searchable register with historical-data labels.

Why no universal acreage or water requirement?

Generation technology, IT density, setbacks, redundancy, cooling and parcel geometry change the answer. Gross acreage is displayed for comparison; developable acreage must come from a survey and engineered test fit. No listed tract passes a land gate solely because it exceeds a selected acreage.

What do the abbreviations mean?

MW: megawatts of electrical power. IT load: power used by computing equipment. PUE: total facility power divided by IT equipment power. MMcf/day: million standard cubic feet of gas per day. Behind the meter: generation serving a facility on its side of the metering boundary; this does not by itself establish grid independence. NTP: notice to proceed.

How should listings be refreshed?

Recheck listing status, asking price and broker statements before outreach or an offer. Replace claims with dated utility studies, agreements and permit records as they become available. This site does not automatically refresh from brokers.

Answers that keep the investigation moving

The questions behind the marketing.

Can a pipeline crossing prove fuel availability?

No. Request the operator’s capacity, pressure, gas-quality and connection analysis. Easements and delivery rights must also be addressed.

Explore the evidence →
Does a 200-acre parcel automatically pass?

No. Gross acreage does not show the buildable shape, drainage, setbacks or equipment layout. Use a phased test fit.

Explore the evidence →
Can closed-loop cooling still use water?

Yes. The internal loop and final heat-rejection system are separate design choices. Obtain a complete campus water balance.

Is an unknown a neutral score?

No. Scoring an unknown as average can make an undocumented site appear competitive. Keep eligibility, commercial attractiveness and evidence completeness distinct.
